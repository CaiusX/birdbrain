Help · how to read this site

BirdBrain listens to the audio of live African wildlife cams — Africam, explore.org, NamibiaCam, PixCams (Majete, Malawi), and a garden microphone — and uses BirdNET to identify birds by their calls, around the clock. Everything you see here is logged automatically: no one is sitting and listening. This page explains how to read it. For credits and citation, see about.

Reading a detection

Spectrogram
The little image on each detection is a spectrogram — a picture of sound. Time runs left → right, pitch low → high (bottom to top), and brightness is loudness. Click any spectrogram to open a player: hear the 3-second clip, watch the playhead sweep across, and see coloured boxes marking where each species likely called. You can compare it against reference recordings from Xeno-Canto, and click the bird's name to open its own page.
Confidence
The number on each detection (0–1) is how sure BirdNET is — higher means more certain. Quiet, distant, or overlapping calls score lower. The site hides anything below a cutoff; the confidence page lets you slide that cutoff and watch the counts change.
Conservation status

Getting around

live
The home dashboard — a map of the cameras, today's summary, and the latest detections as they come in.
sites
Which sites are busiest and what's been heard most over a recent window.
species
Browse every species heard, grouped by site. Click one for its own page with a range map, photo, and when it's active.
confidence
Slide the certainty cutoff and watch how many species and detections survive — a feel for the quality/quantity trade-off.
review
Listen through recent clips one by one, or look at moments where several species overlap in one window.
rare
Unusual, first-time, and conservation-flagged finds worth a second look.
daily
What's vocal at each hour of the day — the dawn chorus and who calls when.
search
The box at the top right jumps straight to any species' page by name.

Good to know

Times and hour-of-day charts are shown in each camera's local timezone, not yours. Automatic identification isn't perfect — a low-confidence ID, an unexpected species, or a clip that doesn't sound right is the model guessing, so trust your ears and the spectrogram. The AI-written site summaries and species notes are generated context, not field records.
