About this site

Real-time bird species recognition from live African wildlife cams. Listens to the audio from Africam, explore.org, NamibiaCam, PixCams (Majete, Malawi), and other African wildlife live-stream channels, runs BirdNET on rolling 3-second windows, and surfaces what it hears here. Hosted on a Raspberry Pi 5.

This is a non-commercial playtest. The BirdNET models that power detection are licensed under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, which permits research, education and personal use but not commercial deployment. Wildlife video remains the property of the originating channels (Africam, explore.org, NamibiaCam, PixCams, and partner lodges); we embed their public streams and analyse only the audio.

Powered by

Africam
Most of the live YouTube wildlife streams in this network — waterholes, lodges, and reserves across Southern and East Africa, plus the Stony Point African Penguin colony via Africam Conservation.
explore.org
Mpala Watering Hole (Laikipia, central Kenya) and other live streams via the Explore African Wildlife channel — Cornell-aligned research-station streams filling biome gaps the Africam roster doesn't reach.
NamibiaCam
Namibia's desert and Etosha live streams — the Namib Desert, the Okaukuejo and Etosha Heights (Safarihoek) waterholes, and the Kalahari cam.
PixCams · Majete Watch
Live cams in Malawi's Majete Wildlife Reserve (lower Shire valley) — solar-powered and streamed over Starlink by PixCams in partnership with African Parks, extending the network into a new region.
BirdNET-Analyzer
Cornell Lab of Ornithology — Kahl et al. The deep-learning model that turns 3‑second audio chunks into species predictions.
BirdNET-Pi — Patrick McGuire
The original Pi-hosted BirdNET project. The "run BirdNET 24/7 on a small box with a useful operator UI" pattern was figured out there first — by hand, long before AI-assisted refactoring made the next iteration cheap. This project stands on that shoulder.
Wikipedia & Wikimedia Commons
Species photos, natural-range maps, and IUCN conservation status icons. Content is CC BY-SA — each image links back to its source page.
Wikidata
Fallback range-map source via the P181 (range‑map image) property when the Wikipedia article doesn't include one.
GBIF
Range maps via the occurrence-density tile API (CC BY 4.0). Used when Wikipedia and Wikidata both lack one — each tile links to the GBIF species page for attribution.
iNaturalist
Final range-map fallback via citizen-science observation density (CC BY-NC). Tiles link back to the iNaturalist taxon page; composes cleanly with our BirdNET CC BY-NC-SA licence.
Open-Meteo
Historical weather context (temperature, wind, conditions) woven into the AI site narratives and daily briefs.
Xeno-Canto
Reference bird recordings shown alongside our detections for ear-training.
OpenStreetMap
Map tiles (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL).
Claude (Anthropic)
Writes the daily soundscape brief, the per-species notes, the per-site narratives, and the per-(species, site) bullets.
Plumbing
yt-dlp, ffmpeg, Leaflet, HTMX, Tailwind, FastAPI.
